## Learning outcomes
Use observable verbs: explain, represent, construct, derive, justify, prove, sketch, diagnose, transfer.

## 1. Problem first (when appropriate)
Present a genuine, accessible problem before explaining the new method. State what the reader should try, the time/effort expected, and what to do if stuck. Avoid giving away the key idea prematurely. For lessons that require prerequisites or a worked example first, state the rationale.

> **Try before reading on:** [Specific task. Ask the reader to record their attempt, drawing, conjecture, or uncertainty.]

## 2. First attempt and reflection
Invite a rough strategy, prediction, example, sketch, or partial solution. Make room for uncertainty. Do not equate failure to solve immediately with failure to learn.

## 3. Intuition and motivating idea
Explain why the problem is interesting or difficult and develop a useful mental model. State where the analogy or intuition stops working.

## 4. Visual and representation bridge
Connect verbal, numerical, tabular, graphical, geometric, and symbolic forms where appropriate. Check each visual mathematically. Embed at least one reader-action prompt at the point where a visualization matters.

**Embedded visual prompt:** [Predict / sketch / complete / reconstruct / transform / describe.]  
**Alternative access route:** [Textual, tabular, tactile, symbolic, or other suitable representation.]

## 5. Precise definitions and notation
List definitions, symbols, domains, units, assumptions, and conventions.

## 6. Formal result, method, or model
State the rule, theorem, procedure, or model precisely. Distinguish definitions, hypotheses, conjectures, exact results, empirical laws, and approximations.

## 7. Construction and justification
Explain why each important operation or step is appropriate. Where suitable, have the reader construct an equation/model, formulate a conjecture, prove a claim, or produce a counterexample.

## 8. Worked examples
Progress from accessible to subtle. Make strategic decisions, checks, and common branches explicit.

## 9. Compare, consolidate, and repair misconceptions
Return to the opening attempt. Compare strategies; explain what worked, what failed, and why. Include a plausible incorrect method and diagnose it where useful.

## 10. Practice ladder
- Recognition and interpretation
- Guided completion
- Independent construction / derivation / proof
- Mixed practice where the method is not named
- Transfer to a changed context or assumption
- Cumulative retrieval

## 11. Boundaries and connections
Include counterexamples, edge cases, limiting cases, assumptions, alternative representations, and links to prior/future concepts.

## 12. Summary and retrieval
Provide questions that can be answered without looking at the page, plus spaced/cumulative revisit suggestions.

## 13. Hints and solutions
Use staged hints; avoid placing the key insight too early. Solutions should explain reasoning, not only give answers.

## 14. Verification record
Link the claim ledger entries, source register entries, code/test results, proof review, and visual checks relevant to this chapter.
