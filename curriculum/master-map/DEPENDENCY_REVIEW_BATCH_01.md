## Review rules

A topic appearing earlier in a curriculum does not automatically make it a prerequisite. For each edge, distinguish:

- **Core required:** without the prerequisite skill, the learner cannot meet the full stated outcome at the intended level (although introductory examples may come earlier).
- **Helpful:** it lowers difficulty or deepens meaning but should not block entry.
- **Repair path:** a learner can enter the target concept and return to this skill only when a diagnostic shows a gap.
- **Contextual link:** useful in a particular application, but not a general prerequisite.
- **Remove:** no defensible dependency has been found.

For every recommendation, the reviewer should ask: *What exact knowledge or action transfers from the prerequisite to the target? Could the target be introduced first or co-taught? What evidence would change this classification?*

## What we should learn from this batch

1. **Several “core” links in v0.1 should likely be downgraded.** Topic order is not enough to justify a hard prerequisite.
2. **A target cluster may need multiple entry routes.** For example, coordinate plotting can start in the first quadrant before extending to all four quadrants.
3. **Broad clusters hide partial prerequisites.** A skill may be required for full mastery but not for an introductory lesson. We should record that distinction rather than force a false all-or-nothing sequence.
4. **Some relationships are contextual, not sequential.** Units are extremely useful in scientific formula work without being necessary for symbolic rearrangement itself.
5. **Diagnosis is a design feature.** A learner who struggles with signed arithmetic may need a targeted repair path, not to repeat an entire prerequisite stage.
